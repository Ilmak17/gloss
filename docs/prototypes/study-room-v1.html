01   Хорошее объяснение

Можно просто слушать.

В этом макете звука нет — показано поведение плеера.

We often call an idea a good explanation because it fits what we already know. But fitting the past is only a beginning. A story can sound convincing without helping us decide what to expect next.

A useful explanation must make a prediction that could turn out to be wrong. This gives us something to check. Before looking at the result, we can say what we expect and what would make us reconsider.

Imagine that two explanations account for the same observation. Repeating that observation may leave us with exactly the same uncertainty. A more useful experiment creates a situation in which the explanations predict different outcomes.

The possibility of being wrong

A prediction is informative when it rules something out. If a claim can accommodate every possible result, a new observation cannot tell us whether the claim is useful. We need to know what would count against it.

This does not mean that one surprising result should immediately overturn an idea. Measurements can fail, and the conditions of a test may matter. The task is to examine those alternatives without protecting our favourite explanation from every challenge.

Learning begins when we can describe both what we believe and what evidence might change our mind.
